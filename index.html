Polymarket's own volume is stated in CONTRACTS, not dollars, and that is a correction to how this page read before. Every volume field the Gamma API exposes (volume, volumeNum, volumeClob, volume1yr) returns the same number, and that number is the sum of contract size. Gamma publishes no cash figure at all. Cash traded is size × price and runs materially lower: measured trade-by-trade across sampled markets it is 1.26x to 2.68x below the contract count, and the multiple depends on where each market traded, so no single conversion recovers it. A binary contract pays $1, so the contract count is the maximum possible payout rather than the money that changed hands. It stays sortable because it's the same basis in every row.

Three money quantities can be sorted here and they are not interchangeable. Traded volume carries a different basis row by row: notional, face notional, handle, limit placed. Each is named beside its figure, never summed and never treated as like-for-like. Addressable revenue is money an operator keeps, and is the one quantity that compares cleanly across rows. Outside signals stay in their own native units inside each row and are never ordered against each other.

Volume and revenue rank these rows almost oppositely, and that is the most useful thing on this page. Exchange take on notional is a fraction of a basis point while betting margin is single-digit percent, so a $2,419T market and a $3.32B market can return similar money. A reader who sorts one way and not the other reaches the opposite conclusion.

Addressable revenue is what an operator would actually earn: GGR for betting, takeout for pari-mutuel, fees for exchanges. Every conversion is shown inside the row with its operands: multiplier, price, day count. Where one is missing, the row names what's missing rather than estimating it.

One exact-product figure is stated as notional rather than cash traded; the row marks it where the figure is explained.

One venue held the variable still. It listed copper, natural gas, corn, wheat, TTF gas and H100 compute at the same time, to the same users, on the same rails. Copper and gas now trade $10.4M and $7.4M a day; corn, wheat, TTF and H100 recorded zero trades and were delisted. Venue constant, demand isolated.

Growth is the live direction, not the longest available CAGR. Several rows grow on a multi-year rate and shrink in the year now running; the chip states the live direction and the opened row carries both, with the window and basis named. Where a series contradicts itself, as M&A does with value at a record while deal count falls three years running, it's marked broken rather than given a rate.

The direction of Polymarket's own volume is shown separately, because on most rows it disagrees with the underlying. Read the disagreement before the rate. One case inverts on inspection: PM's SPX daily volume fell 95% from its February peak, but the market count went 28 to 1 over the same window and volume per market rose to its second-highest level ever. That's a listing decision, not demand decay.

Expected growth is only shown where a named forecaster publishes one. Six rows have none and say so. Nothing here was extrapolated into a forecast, and where the only forward figure is a government policy target rather than a projection, the row says which.

Five rows are priced with a CME rate-per-contract, and that rate is derived rather than printed. CME publishes RPC by product line quarterly, in its 8-K exhibit, and never annually; each figure here is the volume-weighted annual across those four quarters. The weighting reproduces CME's own FY2025 totals to within $0.3M on $4.9B, which is what licenses it. Two limits ride on every one of them: a line-wide rate applied to a single product inside the line is an approximation, since micros and member volume price below the line average and options above; and micro contracts are growing fast enough to move it, with Micro WTI up 209% year on year in Q2 2026 at a hundredth the contract size.

No capture rate is applied anywhere. That judgment is per row, and it belongs to the reader.
